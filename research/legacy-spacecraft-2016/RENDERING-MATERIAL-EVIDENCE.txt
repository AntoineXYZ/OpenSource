2016 spacecraft rendering: material and surface evidence
========================================================

All original binaries were inspected read-only through Ghidra MCP with an
explicit program name. Addresses below refer to the 2016 AuAtom13.dll unless
otherwise stated. No build or original-client visual comparison was run.

Surviving atlas and coordinate table
------------------------------------
src/lib/atom/ui/aubodyrenderwidget.h contains the original SHIP_TEXTURE enum;
the corresponding .cpp ShipTexture function, line 922 at recovery time,
contains the complete original atlas rectangle table. Its comment explicitly
identifies the code as scavenged from DaSpaceShip. The table was checked
against original ShipTexture 0x180466a60, captured in pseudocode/.

The packaged image is :/data/txSpacecraft0Lit.png, registered by
resources/textures/ships/AuShipTex0.qrc. AuBodyRenderWidget already loads it
for its deck backdrop. The actual PNG dimensions are 128x4096; the surviving
source comment claiming 256x8192 is stale. Rectangles use lower-left Y. The
original function adds/subtracts half a pixel to the rectangle endpoints,
then divides U by 128 and V by 4096. Ceiling 29 reverses U.

Useful IDs: 18 HullBottom, 25 HullTop, 26 HullTopBottomWindow, 27 HullWall,
28 HullWallWindow, 29 IntCeiling, 33 IntFloor, 37 IntWallEndCap,
38 IntWallFinished, 39 IntWallFill, 40 IntWallCargo, 41 IntWallFillCargo,
42 IntWallEquipment, 43 IntWallFillEquipment, 48 Railing, 49 RailingPost.

Window alpha is encoded in the atlas. Direct PNG decoding found alpha 25
for 13239/16384 pixels of tile 26 and 14884/16384 pixels of tile 28; the frame
also contains opaque pixels. Applying one constant alpha to each whole
window would discard this distinction. Floor, ceiling, finished/equipment
wall and opaque hull rectangles are opaque. Cargo has a few translucent
edge pixels. The bitmap already contains full surface color, not only an
emission overlay.

Default window opacity (follow-up recovery)
------------------------------------------
RenderEnvironmentObject 0x180423a60 sets the unpowered exterior alpha cutoff
to 0 and the interior cutoff to 0.1 (both fog and non-fog paths). The original
Spacecraft.f.glsl discards alpha <= cutoff. Interior-light/charged-capacitor
branches can use 0.2; the offline preview has no fuel or capacitor charge.
This is directional alpha testing, not ordinary translucent glass blending.
The preview renders surviving window pixels with opaque color/depth, using
the existing outward/inward faces: dark outside, clear inside, beams retained.
It excludes windows from its sorted translucent pass. Other materials keep
their existing alpha handling.

The original 256x8192 txSpacecraft0.png and txSpacecraft0Lit.png resources were
decoded read-only from AuAtomRes13.dll (Qt registration RVA 0x11b0). Window
tiles 26 and 28 are byte-identical in the two atlases, so the embedded original
lit atlas already contains the required exterior pixels. Their dominant glass
RGBA is (6,6,6,25), below the interior 0.1 cutoff. No replacement black image
or removal of the original support-beam artwork is needed. Runtime visual
comparison still requires the user's build.
Read-only pixel checks passed for both atlases: each window tile retains all
65,536 exterior pixels; the interior cutoff retains 8,046 pixels of tile 26
and 4,032 of tile 28. The shader applies the cutoff after texture filtering.

Turret dome texture and opacity correction
-----------------------------------------
RenderTurret 0x18044dca0 and the dome loop in RenderExteriorWalls 0x18042cdb0
both map ST_Turret (56) with UVs (0,0), (0,1), (1,0). The side-wall adapter
incorrectly used the opposite triangular half of this tile for its domes.
All dome orientations now share the recovered glass-half mapping. The wall
corners around side turrets retain their upper-right hull-half mapping.

Turret batches now use the same directional alpha test as hull windows:
opaque surviving exterior pixels, interior cutoff 0.1, no translucent pass.
This also keeps the opaque surrounding wall corners intact. Gun models and
their separate textures are unaffected.

Read-only decoding of the embedded original atlas confirmed 29,247 turret
tile pixels with alpha 25. The glass half is predominantly RGBA (6,6,6,25);
the opposite half, away from the diagonal boundary, is entirely alpha 255.
No build was run; visual verification requires the user's Windows client.

Studio lighting (follow-up recovery)
------------------------------------
AuAtom13.dll 0x1801fb2b0 is shared by DaArena::SetupLights and
DaDesignEnvironment::SetupLights (both exports are present). Ambient is
(0.125,0.125,0.125); three suns lie 10,000ft from the studio origin along:
  (0,0,1),             RGB (220,220,220)
  (-669,-743,-1),      RGB (192,192,192)
  (697,627,-348),      RGB (160,160,160)
Each vector is normalized before multiplying by 10,000. All use quadratic
attenuation 1e-8 and a 1ft disk radius, projected to eye space when rendered.
Modern DaDesignBase::GetRenderLightsRecursive retains these exact constants.
The preview now gets the lights from that controller in studio coordinates,
replacing its camera-following light and 0.48 ambient floor. Moving/turning
the camera no longer rotates the light sources with it.

The custom mesh shader reproduces the recovered vertex diffuse calculation,
solar disk angle, inverse-square attenuation, spherical ambient (15% at the
bottom to 100% at the top), and clamping before texture modulation. Interior
faces use the unpowered InteriorAmbientLight RGB from 0x1800cdcf0:
(0.014352942,0.015,0.014705883). Occupied native consoles receive the same suns
transformed to eye space. Self-lit screens/effect overlays retain their behavior.
This restores surface shading; no new shadow-map or occlusion system is implied.
Per-material specular coefficients have not been recovered for the custom mesh.

Read-only checks matched the modern light constants to the Ghidra result and
evaluated the recovered diffuse equation on opposing surface normals. No shader
compilation, build or original-client visual comparison was run by the assistant.

Interior walls
--------------
RenderInterior 0x18042f410 traverses each room boundary except fuel 9 and
roof 22..24. It splits spans at changed exterior status, changed neighboring
room tags, door locations, and railing-opening details. AdjacentRoomsData
0x1800c4400 samples both sides of the boundary. The renderer keeps low 0x1fff
of each tag (type/subtype); it does not read persistent room IDs here.
No explicit equal-tag wall exclusion was found; normal room merging makes
same-tag adjacent areas one boundary. Open-below 11/15 remain in this loop;
the floor buffer can carry the room tag from the deck underneath them.

Four interior wall material switches use the same map, confirmed from raw
jump-table bytes and disassembly because the full decompilation mislabels
the indirect branches. See pseudocode/interior-material-switch-assembly.txt.

Room types 3 Brig, 5 Hold, 13 FlightDeck select Cargo 40 / CargoFill 41.
Types 8 Engine, 16 Sensor, 17 Shield, 21 Weapon select Equipment 42 / Fill 43.
Other traversable rooms select Finished 38 / Fill 39.

These material switches read the type from AdjacentRoomsData's retained
floor-buffer sample, not from the current room iterator. For example, an
open-below room 15 over engine 8 inherits Equipment on that side. The
current room boundary still defines where partitions occur. Keeping only
sample-tag inequality loses partitions between a hall and an open-below
room whose retained sample also happens to be hall. Both original room
ownership and sampled material tags are needed for a mesh reconstruction.

A span of at most 5 feet uses the narrow fill rectangle. Longer spans use
the main rectangle with U from 0 to ceil(length/10ft) and full 8-foot height.
These are internal partition walls. Hull boundaries use the interior-facing
RenderExterior pass and retain the hull's exterior profile and materials.
AuData13.dll ChaseBoundary 0x18016f830 emits boundary vertices only when the
direction or full room tag changes; it removes a redundant first vertex.
Hull-wall UVs map one complete rectangle over each entire resulting strip;
they do not repeat a tile on each quarter-grid edge.

Hull corner miter table 180a0e750 is initialized partly at runtime by
FUN180071bf0. Its final entries are 0, -cot(67.5deg), -1, -cot(22.5deg), 0,
cot(22.5deg), 1, cot(67.5deg): 0, -0.4142135624, -1, -2.4142135624, 0,
2.4142135624, 1, 0.4142135624. The uninitialized binary contains zero for
the 45-degree cases. Intersecting adjacent offset lines gives the same
miter, with the normal pointing out from the hull boundary.
RenderWallEndCap 0x18044e0f0 uses HullWall 27 for the exterior and
IntWallEndCap 37 for the interior. End-cap V is proportional to height/8.
The original caps each profile against its baseline when neighboring tags
change; joining only the exposed difference between profiles is a geometry
simplification and should be assessed in an original-client comparison.

Horizontal surface predicates
-----------------------------
Each 5.656854249-foot square is sampled as four triangles using quarter-grid
offsets (3,2), (2,3), (1,2), (2,1); each square maps one complete atlas tile.
Let tag be the unsigned 16-bit sample after filling the current deck:

Interior floor 33: tag!=0 and !(tag&0x8000) and !(tag&0x2000), and additionally
either the room is not a glass-bottom room or (tag&0x4000)!=0.

Interior ceiling 29: tag!=0 and !(tag&0x8000) and (tag&0x4000)!=0 and
!(tag&0x2000). It faces down at the current deck plane, serving the room
underneath. The exterior-top reverse pass supplies ceilings where there is
no next-deck room. RenderInteriorFloors' second call is a hatch-only pass.

Exterior bottom: tag!=0 and !(tag&0x8000) and !(tag&0x4000).
Exterior top: tag!=0 and (tag&0x8000)!=0. The retained prior-deck tag supplies
the type/armor-dependent material, at the next deck plane.

Glass-bottom/top rooms: Bridge 2 and Lounge 14 always; Weapon 21 if armor<8;
Captain 4 if armor<7. These use HullTopBottomWindow 26. Others use bottom 18
or top 25. The interior-facing exterior pass omits opaque bottoms (already
covered by floor 33) but includes reversed top 25 and window 26.

These predicates are directly visible in RenderInteriorFloors 0x180435fd0,
RenderInteriorCeilings 0x180432f60, RenderExteriorBottoms 0x1804274c0 and
RenderExteriorTops 0x18042a140. The latter two captures were added here.

Floor buffer flags must be retained across decks. Original AuData13.dll
FillFloor 0x1801713a0 special-cases open-below 11/15: over no prior coverage
write current tag; over previous fuel 9 write current tag|0x4000; over other
prior coverage preserve previous low tag|0x6000. After a deck, discard stale
0x8000 samples, clear 0x2000, set 0x8000 on remaining occupied samples.

Hatches and turrets can cut holes at corners, edge midpoints and centers.
The plane routines test IsAnyHatch or detail 22/33, then generate dedicated
pieces around their openings. RenderHatch 0x18042ee90 confirms square-center
holes are axis-aligned squares extending 2ft in X/Y. At corners and edge
midpoints they are diamonds with vertices 2.8284271245ft from the center on
each cardinal axis. Treating an entire triangle as missing removes too much
surface. IsAnyHatch covers types 8, 51, 52 and 77..97.

RenderHatch chooses half-open atlas 35 for types 8/77..83, 15 for 51/84..90,
and 23 for hull hatches. A per-door animation index controls the actual
displayed state. Closed equivalents are 34, 14, 22; using those in a static
designer preview is a deliberate static-state choice, not recovered
animation behavior.

The original wall/floor aperture lookups do not filter detail.hidden at +2.
RenderExterior has no read of that byte; the saved top/bottom routines match
deck, type and location directly. IsDetailAt 0x1803f8f40 does the same.
RenderInterior checks +2 only in its final furnished-detail loop, starting
at 0x18043290a. Hidden equipment is skipped there. Including hidden doors
and hatches in the surface aperture pass therefore follows the recovered
consumers. See pseudocode/detail-hidden-surface-evidence.txt.

Wall apertures
--------------
Small internal doors 5/49/50 have half-width 2.5ft and height 8ft. Type 5 uses
interior texture 31 and 49/50 crew texture 12 (both half-open animation base;
closed variants 30 and 11). Internal large doors 53/54 and openings 55/56
replace a complete run between direction/adjacency changes. 53 uses large
door 3, 54 uses large crew door 6; 55 has no wall; 56 emits a 3ft-high railing
with a 0.3ft top rail. Closed large-door texture IDs are 2 and 5.

RenderExterior inserts virtual room types for wall details. Small HullDoor 50
is centered with half-width 2.5ft and virtual type 30. Its WallProfile is
(offset 0, height 0, strip 2), (0,7,strip 4), (1,7,strip 5 if armor<=8 else 4),
(0,8). LargeHullDoor 3 / HullOpening 9 span the existing simplified wall
segment minus 1ft at each end, virtual types 28 and 29 respectively. 28 has a
single strip 1 from 0 to 8ft; 29 is strip 0 and emits no face. SideTurret
21/34..40 uses half-width 2.8284271245ft. Hull strip 1 uses large-hull-door 9
and strip 2 uses hull-door 20 (half-open animation base; closed 8/19).

These dimensions were checked in assembly because the decompiler loses
several floating-point call arguments: wall-opening-assembly.txt captures
the original range calculations and constant addresses 180795810(2.5) and
180795818(2.8284271245). Dedicated hatch/turret/door geometry still requires
animation and furnished-detail handling beyond the plane geometry itself.

Streamline and roof recovery limits
----------------------------------
No streamline read or branch was found in CreateModels, RenderExterior,
RenderExteriorWalls, RenderExteriorTops or RenderExteriorBottoms. The wall
generator's design-header accesses found here are armor at +0x15 passed to
WallProfile. The earlier SURFACE-RECOVERY-NOTES.txt statement about
streamline-dependent paths was not established by these functions and
must not justify inventing a streamline deformation.

The flag's recovered consumers confirm its physics role: DesignStreamline
0x1803e5a80 is called by HullStress0x1803f54e0, MaxAtmosphereSpeed0x180403350,
MaxAtmosphereThrottle0x180403440, and the UI ShipDesignStreamline accessor.
A complete instruction search for cached field +0x841 found only its
AnalyzeDesign writer and DesignStreamline reader. Streamlined hulls use
one quarter of the unstreamlined drag-squared stress factor and 512 versus
256 in atmospheric speed/throttle limits. SetDesignStreamline 0x1804618b0
updates the design field and invalidates analysis/path state. Its caller
list and physics functions are captured in streamline-physics-consumers.txt.

The interior boundary loop excludes roof 22..24. The spacecraft exterior
still runs its occupancy/profile pipeline for those records; their full
building-style roof semantics are not established by this interior study.
